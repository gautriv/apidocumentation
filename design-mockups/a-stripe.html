Lesson 14 — Writing docs for LLMs

~9 min read · Maya · Module 7 — AI-native documentation

It's 4 p.m. on a Thursday, friend. A developer Slacks you: "hey what does 429 mean again 😅". You answer them in five seconds. Now imagine that same question, three thousand times a day, asked by an agent.

The good news: writing for LLMs is mostly just writing well, with the dial turned up on a few specific things. Predictable structure. Errors that contain the recovery action. No ironic asides about how flaky the rate-limiter is.

The slightly harder news is that "writing well" is the part most teams skip, because the API ships first and the docs ship "soon." So this lesson is going to feel like it's about LLMs, and it kind of is, but really it's about a discipline you should have had anyway.

what's the difference between 429 and 503?
Maya

429 means "you, specifically, are over your rate limit." The fix is on your side — back off, then retry.

503 means "the service is gasping — for everyone." The fix is the server's; you wait and retry with backoff.

In your docs, never blur them. An agent reading 429 will (correctly) sleep their key; an agent reading 503 will (correctly) wait + retry the same call.
